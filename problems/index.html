Finding problems

  • Methods: every problem is tagged by the methods its solutions use. Pick tags in the Methods list in the filters (type a name or a number to find one). All selected (AND) needs every tag; Any selected (OR) needs one. Clicking a tag on any problem also filters by it.
  • Search: problem names (“2019 C6”, “USAMO 2021”), method names or words from the classification.
  • Filters: contest, subject, years, difficulty (a rough 1–5 estimate; see Coverage & caveats) and your progress (not done, done, or due to revisit).
  • Random problem opens a problem that matches your filters, preferring ones you haven’t done. Copy search link saves the current search as a link.

A problem’s page

  • Click a problem’s name to see its statement, its methods, and the sources (AoPS, Official).
  • No spoilers: Hide hides the method tags (for every problem, until you turn it off). Then Hint: primary method and Hint: other methods reveal them step by step.
  • Classification explains how the methods are used; Research notes & caveats lists the posts that were read.
  • Focus shows only the statement and your notes, in larger text (Esc to leave).
  • Revisit: under “Your notes”, pick in a week (or another date) to come back to a problem. When it’s due, a note at the top of the page tells you.

Your progress

  • Select lets you tick problems (or Select all) and then mark them done or add them to a collection.
  • The Progress grid shows one contest at a time: a square per problem, green when done. Filters highlight the matching squares. Click a square for its menu; in Select mode, drag across squares to select a block.

Collections & practice

  • A collection is your own list of problems, such as “Geometry to review” or a weekly set. Make one in the Collections tab, then add problems with Select, from a grid square’s menu, or with Add problems by name (“2019 ISL C6, USAMO 2021/3”).
  • Drag ⠿ to reorder, Sort… to sort once, and Add section for headings like “Week 1”. Note adds a short reminder to a problem.
  • Done here marks a problem done in that collection only, so you can redo a set without touching your overall progress.
  • Practice goes through the collection one problem at a time, with the methods hidden and a timer. Print makes a problem set (or a PDF).
  • Public gives a link anyone can open (signed-in users can make one); others can Save a copy.

Notes & notebook

  • Your notes on a problem are private and save as you type. LaTeX works ($x^2$), and [\hide="Solution"] … [\hide] hides a spoiler behind a click.
  • The notebook (the book in the corner) holds notes that aren’t about one problem, in tabs. Drag it anywhere; problem names you write in it, like “2019 ISL C6”, become links.

Signing in & offline

  • Without signing in, everything is saved in this browser. Sign in (top right) emails you a link; no password. Then your marks, collections and notes follow you to any device.
  • Once you’ve opened the site, it keeps working offline; changes you make are sent when you’re back online.
